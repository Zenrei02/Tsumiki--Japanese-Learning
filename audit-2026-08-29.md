### 1.3 The section-accent palette states a contrast bar three of its six colours miss

`naoshi-app/src/lib/tokens.js`, `ACCENT`, added Aug 27. The comment block says:

> CONTRAST: these are used as RULES AND BARS ONLY, never behind text, so the 3:1
> non-text contrast bar is what applies rather than 4.5:1.

Measured (WCAG 2.1 relative luminance, against both `T.paper` #F7F6F2 and `T.sheet`
#FFFFFF):

| section | hex | vs paper | vs sheet | 3:1? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hiragana | `#CFC4E6` | 1.53 | 1.65 | **FAIL** |
| katakana | `#A493CE` | 2.54 | 2.75 | **FAIL** |
| checker | `#C9C6BE` | 1.58 | 1.71 | **FAIL** |
| grammar | `#2F6F6B` | 5.38 | 5.82 | pass |
| kanji | `#5B4A7D` | 7.14 | 7.72 | pass |
| vocabulary | `#8A5A3B` | 5.38 | 5.82 | pass |

**This is very probably not a real accessibility defect, and that is the point.** Usage
was read rather than assumed: `App.jsx` uses the accent in exactly two places — a 4px
`borderTop` on each home card (L109) and a 3px `borderBottom` on the section header
(L257). Every section is also named in English and Japanese text right beside the rule.
So the accents convey nothing by hue alone, which means WCAG 1.4.11 arguably does not
apply to them **at all** — they are decorative, not "required to understand the content".

The defect is in the sentence, not the palette. The comment invokes 3:1 as "what
applies", then two lines later gives the reason it may not apply. A later session
reading only the first half will believe these colours were checked against a bar they
miss by a factor of two. Two clean resolutions, both cheap:

- **(a)** reword the comment to say the accents are decorative and redundant with the
  text labels, therefore exempt from 1.4.11 — and note that the exemption is what the
  "never behind text" rule is protecting; or
- **(b)** keep the bar and darken the three. `#CFC4E6` → roughly `#8E7BB8` and
  `#C9C6BE` → roughly `#8F8B80` clear 3:1 while staying inside the 藤 family and the
  deliberately-quiet grey. This costs the "lightest shade" reading of hiragana, which
  the comment argues for on purpose, so (a) is likely the better answer.

Either way the fix is in `build-vite-app.py` (which authors `ACCENT`), never in the
generated `tokens.js`. **Not a Japanese-content question — Lloyd's call alone.**

### The esbuild harness lied about its own controls, and the pass still stands

Worth recording because it is this project's signature failure in a new place.

First run reported **both negative controls exiting 0** — a harness that cannot fail.
The controls were fine; the *reporting* was broken:

```bash
"$EB" ... "$f" >/dev/null 2>&1; echo "control $(basename $f) exit=$?"   # WRONG
```

`$(basename $f)` runs a subshell during argument expansion and resets `$?`, so the line
printed basename's exit code, not esbuild's. Same family as the piped-command masking
the brief warns about, and as `--loader=jsx` failing for the wrong reason on Aug 15.

Re-run with `rc=$?` captured on its own line before any substitution: both controls
exit **1**, plus a third control (a real file, `tokens.js`, copied and deliberately
broken) also exits 1. Only then were the 23 passes believed. **The pass was never in
doubt on the evidence — the evidence was.**

One genuine-looking FAIL was also mine: `shared-stroke-engine.js` contains JSX but is
`.js`, so `--loader:.jsx=jsx` does not reach it. Under `--loader:.js=jsx` it compiles
clean. Not a defect in the file.
