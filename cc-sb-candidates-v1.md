# CC / SB candidates — flagged, not added (v1, 2026-08-13)

Session 13 standing rule from Lloyd: as authoring passes move through the
grammar, flag points that deserve a Culture Connection or Skill Builder but
do NOT add them without review. **Auto-qualifier: anything with no English
equivalent.** This file is the flag queue; each entry names the attach point
so adding one later is a placement, not a search.

Added this session (for contrast, already in the module): sb-no, cc-n
(Step 3); cc-nakya, sb-must (Step 5); cc-chau, sb-aspect (Step 13 / N4).

## Flagged candidates

1. **ね・よ・よね — the sentence-final tuning particles.** SB. Attach: after
   `yo` (Step 1 area, both lessons exist). English does all of this with
   tone of voice — auto-qualifies. The drill: same sentence, three endings,
   three different social moves; the mistake is よ where ね belonged
   (informing someone of their own feelings).

2. **Counters and their sound shifts.** SB. Attach: after `counters`
   (Step 3). No English counter system — auto-qualifies. Drill: choosing the
   counter (つ/人/枚/本/匹) and the sound changes (一本 いっぽん, 三本
   さんぼん, 一人 ひとり). The existing lesson teaches the idea; the SB would
   drill the trap pairs.

3. **こそあど as a THREE-way split.** SB. Attach: after `kosoado` (Step 1).
   English has two distances; Japanese has three plus the の-series — the
   lesson's own build already calls これ/この mixing "the classic error",
   which is an SB begging to exist.

4. **お/ご honorific prefixes.** CC. Attach: Step 5-ish, or beside the kanji
   module's お茶 (Group 4 already teaches the word). No English equivalent —
   auto-qualifies. Why お茶 but not おビール; which words fossilized the
   prefix (ごはん, お金); why stripping it can sound rough.

5. **Family terms and uchi/soto — 母 vs お母さん.** CC. Attach: wherever
   family vocabulary lands (kanji module already glosses 母 as "my mother",
   which quietly assumes this system). Humble-for-mine / honorific-for-yours
   has no English equivalent — auto-qualifies. High error rate in real
   learner speech (calling one's own mother お母さん to an outsider).

6. **あいづち — the listening noises.** CC. Attach: Stage 2 opener region.
   うん・へえ・そうですか・なるほど; English back-channels exist but the
   density and social weight don't transfer. Recognition + when silence
   reads as coldness.

7. **Casual particle-dropping.** CC. Attach: Stage 2, near cc-chau —
   コーヒー飲む？ ご飯食べた？ Speech drops は/を/か freely; learners either
   never notice or over-copy it into writing. Pairs naturally with the
   clipped-forms thread (cc-nakya, cc-chau).

8. **The trailing 〜けど / 〜が.** CC or SB. Attach: after `ga-but`
   (Step 11). A sentence deliberately left hanging as a softener
   (ちょっと高いんですけど…) — English trailing "but…" reads unfinished;
   Japanese reads polished. No equivalent — auto-qualifies.

9. **〜そう triangle (looks-about-to / hearsay / agreement).** SB. Attach:
   Stage 2, when 〜そうです lessons are authored. 降りそうです vs
   降るそうです vs そうですね — three near-identical shapes, three meanings;
   classic N4 confusion set. Flag now so the lessons get authored WITH the
   SB slot in mind.

10. **Set phrases with no propositional content — いただきます,
    ごちそうさま, おつかれさま, よろしくお願いします.** CC. Attach: Stage 2
    or a culture strand. Untranslatable ritual language — auto-qualifies;
    おつかれさま alone is half of office Japanese.
