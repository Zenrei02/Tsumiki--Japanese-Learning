WHAT THIS SCREEN IS FOR

Getting a newcomer to the right first block in one tap — two for the hiragana reader. It is not a tour. It replaces Home's content (header and drawer stay), shows once, and every way out dismisses it for good.

DECIDED

  • "I can already read hiragana" offers Katakana AND Kanji, side by side as equals (Lloyd, Oct 3). Same block, same size, each with its existing Home line; neither preselected.
  • No red on this screen. The kit's rule is that the one red thing is the thing to do next — here there are three equally right answers, so none is lacquer.
  • No counts, no time estimates, no romaji. The only new Japanese is the greeting ようこそ.

OPEN — LLOYD'S CALL

  • All English copy — every line marked DRAFT, the three answer lines included. The Katakana and Kanji lines in frame 2 are the shipped HOME_WHY text, unchanged.
  • The greeting. ようこそ beside WELCOME, or no Japanese at all in the header.
  • Skip placement. "Just look around" sits in the top card so it is visible without scrolling on a short phone. The alternative is a full-width button under the answers.
  • The scene is not a door here. On Home, tapping the room goes to the room. On the welcome it would be a fourth exit that skips the question, so it is a picture only.

BEHAVIOUR

  • Shown when nothing is started and the welcome has not been seen, and only after progress has loaded, so a returning learner never sees it flash.
  • Seen-flag tsumiki-welcome-v1 is in KEYS so it follows an account. Its value is the same on every device, so a sign-in never asks "which copy?" about it.
  • Sign in marks it seen and opens the existing Account panel. A returning learner's progress then syncs down and Home is theirs.
  • In a private window storage is memory-only, so the welcome comes back on reload. Accepted.
