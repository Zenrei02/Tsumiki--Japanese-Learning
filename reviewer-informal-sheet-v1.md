## 1. Levels of politeness

We teach five ways to ask for a pen. We tell learners to choose the level by
**who they are talking to** — not by how much they want the pen.

| Level | Japanese | We teach it for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ペン、貸して。 | a close friend |
| 2 | ペンを貸してくれる？ | a friend, a little softer |
| 3 | ペンを貸してもらえますか。 | a coworker — "normal at work" |
| 4 | ペンをお貸しください。 | formal |
| 5 | ペンをお貸しいただけないでしょうか。 | someone much higher than you |

**Questions:**

- Is the order correct?
- Is level 3 normal between coworkers?
- We tell learners: **too polite is also a mistake.** We say level 5 to a friend
  sounds sarcastic, or sounds like you are angry. Is this true?

## 2. 〜くせに

We call this **the rudest way to say "even though."** We say it is like のに,
but with contempt.

> 知っているくせに、教えてくれませんでした。
> 子どものくせに、生意気です。

We tell learners: **read it, but do not use it.**

We also say 子どものくせに is **never kind**. We say it means *you are only a
child, so how dare you.*

**Questions:**

- Is "contempt" correct? Or does くせに have softer uses too?
- Can 子どものくせに be said in a warm or joking way between close people?
- Is "read it, do not use it" the right advice?

---

## 3. 〜わけだ and 〜わけではない

We say that most English books explain わけだ wrongly. They call it a *reason*.
We say it is not a reason. We say it is a **conclusion** — the listener already
knows the reason, and now they understand.

> 十年住んでいるんですか。道理で日本語が上手なわけですね。
> 電車が止まっていたんです。だから遅れたわけです。

For わけではない, we teach that it says **"that is not the conclusion"** — not
"that is not true."

> 全部わかったわけではありません。
> We teach this as: *I did not understand everything.*
> We say it does **not** mean: *I understood nothing.*

**Questions:**

- Is "conclusion, not reason" correct? Or are we making it too complicated?
- We say わけだ **cannot start a conversation**. Is that true?
- Is our meaning for 全部〜わけではありません correct?

## 4. にあたって and に際して

We teach both as formal. We say にあたって is used a little **earlier** — for
something that is about to begin.

> 面接を受けるにあたって、会社のことをよく調べた。
> ご契約に際して、以下の書類が必要です。

Two things we are not sure about:

- **We say both are wrong for bad events.** We say 事故にあたって is wrong.
- **We say に際して is too formal for small daily things.** We say 昼ご飯に際して
  sounds like a joke.

**Questions:**

- Are those two points correct?
- Is the difference between にあたって and に際して clear to you? Or are they
  almost the same?

---

## 5. 〜かねます

We teach this as a **polite way to refuse**. We say you hear it from shop and
office staff. We teach かねない as the opposite meaning: *it might happen* (and
it is something bad).

> その質問にはお答えしかねます。
> このままでは、大きな問題になりかねない。

**Questions:**

- Do staff really say お答えしかねます and 分かりかねます? Or is it too formal now?
- We say かねない is **never used for good things**. We say 合格しかねない is
  wrong. Is that always true?

## 6. Saying no without いいえ

**This is the most important question. Please be direct with us.**

This lesson is about how Japanese people refuse things. Many foreign books explain
this badly. We may also be wrong.

We do **not** say "Japanese people never say no." We say something different:

- Japanese people say no often. But they usually do not use the word **いいえ**.
- いいえ is for **facts**. 「これはペンですか」「いいえ、鉛筆です」. We say people do
  not use いいえ to refuse a request or to disagree.
- We say these are the real ways to refuse:
  - 「明日はちょっと…」 — the sentence is not finished, and that *is* the refusal
  - 「難しいですね」 — this means no
  - 「考えておきます」 or 「検討します」 — sometimes real, often no
- We give a reason: this way, **nobody has to hear the word no.** The person who
  asked is not embarrassed.

**Questions:**

- Is anything here too strong, or too confident?
- Is 難しいですね really a clear no?
- Is our reason correct? Or is it a foreign idea that is too simple?
- **Is there anything here you would delete?**

---

## 7. Two rules — quick yes or no

We teach these to beginners as rules with **no exceptions**.

- **まえに never uses past tense.** Always 寝るまえに, even about the past.
- **ながら needs one person doing both actions.** For two people, we say you must
  use 〜ている間に.

**Are both always true? Or should we make them softer?**
