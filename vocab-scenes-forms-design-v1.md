# Vocabulary — scenes and verb forms (design v1)

*Session 18, Aug 20 2026. Lloyd's brief: give vocabulary the Session 17 vibe —
the lesson arcs' lived-moment openers — and make verbs carry their forms, with
the forms turning into questions once the grammar has taught them, N3 forms
arriving as they are triggered, and a pool of common verbs sampled at random
for review whenever a new form unlocks.*

## 1. Scenes — the word card opens on a moment

Every scene is a **place the learner will actually stand**: the konbini
register, the clinic reception desk, a platform announcement half-caught. Shape:

```json
{ "scene": "EN vignette, 2–3 sentences, second person, present tense",
  "jp": "one Japanese line containing the word",
  "en": "its translation",
  "src": "grammar:<point-id>" | "new" }
```

Rendered at the **top of the word card**, before "Meet this word" — the
situation opens the word exactly as the arc's setting opens a grammar lesson.
No dismiss control, no read-state (same ruling as the anecdotes).

**The JP line is reused wherever possible.** Most scene-worthy words already
appear in a grammar example sentence, and those lines are in the reviewer's
queue via the grammar batches. A reused line adds zero reviewer load; `src`
records the point it came from. New lines are marked `src: "new"` and go to the
reviewer. Wave 1 is ~30 words, weighted toward the N3 admin register (clinic,
counters, workplaces) where the scene does the most connecting work.

Scenes and the micro-anecdotes are different organs: the anecdote is a *fact
about Japan* (sourced, claim-checked); the scene is a *fictional moment* (no
claims, no sources needed — only the Japanese needs review). Both can sit on
one card; the scene leads, the anecdote stays in its "Out in the world" card.

## 2. Verb forms — the ladder follows the grammar

Verb classes are **derived, not authored**: UniDic classifies every ledger verb
(godan by row / ichidan / する / 来る), and the derivation correctly catches
the fake-ichidan traps 帰る・入る・知る as godan — the exact distinction
sb-verbtypes teaches. Conjugation is **rule-generated** by a pure function in
the module, with an exception table (行く→行って, ある→ない, する/来る
suppletion). Dictionary facts by rule; the unit test pins ~40 golden forms.

The ladder, each form tied to the grammar point that teaches it:

| form | grammar point | step |
|---|---|---|
| ます | masu | 4 |
| て | te | 4 |
| plain past た | ta-plain | 4 |
| plain negative ない | naidekudasai | 5 |
| past negative なかった | naidekudasai | 5 |
| potential | potential | 10 |
| たら | tara | 12 |
| passive | passive | 12 |
| ば | ba | 16 |
| volitional | you-vol | 17 |
| causative | saseru | 20 |
| causative-passive | saserareru | 20 |
| imperative | meirei | 20 |
| 〜はじめる | hajimeru | 32 (N3) |
| 〜かける | kakeru | 32 (N3) |
| 〜ず | zuniwairarenai | 40 (N3) |

**Display rule (Lloyd's brief, verbatim logic):** a verb card always shows its
forms table. A form whose grammar point is not yet done renders as a plain
reference row — the form is *there*, readable, never hidden. A form whose
grammar point IS done (quizBest/studied + practiced, the grammar module's own
isDone) renders as a **question first**: "て form?" with four choices, answer
revealed on tap. Distractors are generated by mis-applying the other class's
rule — 帰る offers 帰て (the ichidan mistake) precisely because that is the
mistake sb-verbtypes exists to prevent. Answering right or wrong reveals the
row; nothing gates progress. N3 forms join the table only once triggered, so
the table grows with the learner.

## 3. The review pool

`FORM_POOL` — common corpus verbs chosen to span every conjugation rule:

- godan う-row: 買う・会う・使う  · く: 行く(irregular て)・書く・聞く
- ぐ: 急ぐ  · す: 話す  · つ: 待つ  · む: 飲む・読む・休む
- る (real): 作る・分かる  · **る (traps): 帰る・入る・知る**
- ichidan: 食べる・見る・起きる・開ける・忘れる
- する: 勉強する  · 来る  · ある (ない-exception)

**When a new form unlocks** (its grammar point flips to done since the last
visit — tracked in `known-words-v1` under `_formsSeen`, no new storage key),
the module builds a **form drill**: a stratified random sample of six —
one ichidan, one plain godan, one る-trap, one する or 来る, 行く or ある if
their exception touches the form, plus one wild — each asked as an MCQ in the
new form. Drills appear as a card on Today, pay no points (consistent with the
achievement row: drills earn nothing), and record completion per form.

## 4. Kanji — the same standard, without re-opening etymology

The anecdote pass deliberately kept culture out of the kanji module (character
stories drift into etymology, the module's flagged reviewer risk). The device
that fits: **"In the wild"** — one line per lesson naming where in Japan the
learner will *see* these characters: 営業中 on a shop door, 駐車場 on a sign,
自由席 on a shinkansen ticket, 最後尾 held up at the end of a queue,
関係者以外立入禁止 on a gate. Observable, checkable, travel-anchored — the
scene idea applied to the streetscape, with no historical claims at all.
Pilot on Group 7 (this session); backfill for Groups 0–6 is a follow-up wave.

## Reviewer load from this design

New JP scene lines (`src:"new"` only), the "In the wild" sightings (checkable
by any resident), and nothing else — conjugation is rule-generated and the
reused scene lines are already in the queue.
